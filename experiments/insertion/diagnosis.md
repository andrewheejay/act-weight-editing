# Diagnosis, insertion, original policy

Checkpoint: `stage0/checkpoint.json` (hub revision 33259aa8). Numbers come from `python experiments/insertion/stage1/report_ins.py stats`, which writes `stage1/ins_pattern.json`, and from `analysis_ins.py stats <run>` (`ins_stats_<run>.json`), both from the committed per-seed files and `features_*.jsonl.gz`. Key names are in brackets. Rebuilding the features needs the trace cache, which is not in git. Review round 1 (reviews/insertion-g1-r1.json) changed this file. The parts marked [R1] are new or corrected, and the old wording is gone. The new numbers are in `stage1/ins_review1.json` (`review1_ins.py`, from committed files), `ins_reach.json` and `ins_reach_matched.json` (`reach_ins.py`), and per-episode files `depth_*.jsonl.gz`, `runs_default.jsonl.gz`, `reach*_default.jsonl.gz`. They need the trace cache only to rebuild, not to read. Data: design seeds 20000 to 20999 at the default setting, the same seeds at chunks of 50 and 25 and with ensembling (first 200 seeds), and two 41 by 41 grids (1,681 rollouts each, DEVIATIONS.md 2026-11-25). No validation seed has been run. Strict success is the primary score (below), so "failure" here means no strict success.

The grids use the same policy on other positions. They replicate across positions, they are not new policies, and grid points lie millimetres from each other. The chunk runs are the same design seeds.

## Target failure

| Group | What the episode reached (stock reward, highest) | Count of 1000 | Share of the 926 strict failures |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | strict success | 74 | |
| A | class 4a, the peg touched the pin, never strict | 114 | 12% |

The target is **class 3, the M group**: 405 of 1000 seeds (40.5%, Wilson 95% interval 37.5% to 43.6%). Benchmark success 18.8% (188, Wilson 16.5% to 21.3%) and strict success 7.4% (74, 5.9% to 9.2%).

Why M and not another group.
- By SPEC's classes it is the largest single class (405 against 205, 159, 114 and 43).
[R1] The earlier text set the grasp classes aside as "mixtures of causes" with "no one arm or step". That is wrong. Split by which object was not lifted (z above 0.06 m):
- **Right arm, peg not lifted only: 214 seeds** (class 0 39, class 1 152, class 2 23). Median deviation of the right gripper from the mean successful position at the first close 3.27 cm against 1.00 cm in S (AUC 0.945 against S, 0.852 against all episodes that met). **Left arm, socket not lifted only: 120 seeds** (class 0 94, class 1 23, class 2 3), 4.17 cm against 1.25 (AUC 0.985 and 0.973). Neither lifted: 52. Both lifted: 21. The cut at 0.055 or 0.07 changes these counts by at most 5. The right-arm part fails 21% of the design seeds, clustered 2.5 times above its base rate (52.8% given a neighbour of the same part against 21.4%), the left 12%, 4.0 times (47.5% against 12%). The rates by position fifth are for the left part by socket y 0.15, 0.21, 0.155, 0.035, 0.05 (the low-y half of the table) and by socket x 0.065, 0.09, 0.14, 0.195, 0.11. For the right part by peg y 0.11, 0.27, 0.30, 0.22, 0.19 and flat in peg x (0.175 to 0.24).
- These deviations are not contact deflection. [R1, `ins_reach_matched.json`] At the first step where the commanded gripper is less than 10 cm above its object (the commanded joint targets, by kinematics, checked against the traced gripper centre to 4e-8 m), the commanded lateral offset is 3.13 cm in the right-arm part against 1.32 cm in S (AUC 0.899, all 209 episodes of the part have not touched the object yet), and 2.88 cm against 1.48 (AUC 0.845) in the left-arm part (79 of 120 untouched, the rest had touched it already). So the deviation is already in the command before any contact. I read it as an aim error, but a wider approach path at 10 cm height was not separated from it. At the close the measured and commanded deviations are similar (right: 3.27 measured, 3.92 commanded; left: 4.17 and 4.43). Touched before the close in 97% of the right part and 99% of the left part, 57% and 72% in S. I did not work out why the failing episodes touch earlier.
- Class 4a (114): pin contact, not strict. Not the same failure as M. The peg-tip misalignment does not separate it from the strict successes (AUC 0.553, Tracking number below), and it is the closest near miss in the data. [R1] The longest run of the full strict condition (pin contact, right finger on peg, left finger on socket, no table) is 14 steps in the median (p10 5, p90 22) against the 25 required, 56 of 114 reach 15 steps and 29 reach 20 (S: p10 27, median 43). The first longest run ends because the pin contact is lost in 76 of 114, because the episode ends in 25, because a finger leaves in 13 (right 6, left 7), at the table in 0. 11 of the 114 have a pin contact run of 25 steps or more and still fail the strict rule because a hand or table condition breaks the run. A rescued 4a episode is worth a full strict success (value 1.0). It is not the target because its number is small (114) and the tip number does not see it, not because it is far from success.
- M (class 3) and the stage arithmetic. [R1] The chain is 593 of 1000 met, 188 pin, 74 strict (P(met) 0.593, P(pin given met) 0.317, P(strict given pin) 0.394). Upper bounds on strict if a whole stage were fixed and the later rates stayed as they are (an assumption I have not tested): every episode meets, 12.5%; every met episode reaches the pin, 23.3%; every pin contact becomes strict, 18.8% (from 7.4%). The local estimate for a rescued grasp episode, the strict rate of the 10 nearest seeds that met, is 0.106 (0.111 with 20) and the pin rate 0.30 (0.32). So one rescued grasp episode is worth about 0.11 strict, one M episode that reaches the pin about 0.39. M (405 episodes) has the largest bound of the three and the largest value per rescue among the large groups. That is the reason M is the target, and not evidence that M is a near miss (see Near-miss).
- M is not held to one description either: 81 of 405 meet at step 350 or later, 163 get the tip past the mouth plane by 5 mm and of these 141 pass outside the opening (below). In the M group 18.3% and 33.1% have a pre-contact tip offset within 1.5 and 2 times the 0.8 cm limit.
- M and A together are the episodes that meet but do not finish (519). They are not merged because the tip number works only for M.

Whether an edit can fix M is unknown. Two reasons for doubt. The signed offset is not a constant bias (below), and the start-position dependence is not linear and only partly smooth (Pattern, [R1]), so there is no simple position rule to copy. Not claimed: that M is close to succeeding.

## Tracking number

`pre_tip_lat`: one control step before the first peg-socket contact off the table (t_meet - 1), the larger of the two absolute offsets, across the socket axis, of the peg's leading end (the end of the peg nearest the socket centre) from the socket's centre, in the socket frame, in cm. The peg can touch the pin only if its tip gets inside the opening, and the geometry leaves 0.8 cm on each side (half-gap 1.8 cm minus peg half-width 1.0 cm, constants in `awe/measure.py`). Defined only for episodes that meet (593 of 1000 in the design run). In all 593 no peg-socket contact is recorded before t_meet (`first_pegsock` < t_meet in 0).

| Run | AUC M vs S | AUC M vs S and A | AUC A vs S | median tip offset S / A / M (cm) | M within 0.8 cm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| design default (n 74, 114, 405) | 0.843 (bootstrap 95% 0.786 to 0.896) | 0.803 | 0.553 | 0.80 / 0.94 / 2.05 | 4.4% |
| chunk 50 | 0.879 | 0.830 | 0.571 | 0.64 / 0.78 / 1.85 | 6.0% |
| chunk 25 | 0.909 | 0.840 | 0.563 | 0.63 / 0.71 / 1.83 | 11.3% |
| ensembling, 200 seeds | 0.919 | 0.879 | 0.540 | 0.68 / 0.80 / 2.19 | 6.6% |
| grid, peg moved | 0.800 | 0.731 | 0.631 | 0.68 / 1.18 / 1.96 | 3.5% |
| grid, socket moved | 0.658 | 0.607 | 0.568 | 1.34 / 1.51 / 1.59 | 6.8% |

Numbers recomputable with `ins_pattern.json` `tracking`. The last row is the weak spot. With only the socket moved the tip offset barely separates M from S, and the S median there is 1.34 cm, not 0.80. The grid is a conditional slice with the peg always at the centre of its box, so it may miss the configurations where M has large offsets. I did not test that. The tracking number is therefore confirmed on the runs where both objects vary and on the peg grid, and not on the socket grid.

How it was chosen. I computed several numbers on the same design seeds, all in the features file: the centre-based lateral offset (pre_lat, AUC M vs S 0.816), the angle (pre_ang, 0.539), the depth reached after contact (post_min_tip_ax, 0.772), the tip offset (this one, 0.843), and the radial version of the tip offset (0.835). The tip version was chosen on physical grounds (the tip is what has to enter, and the 0.8 cm half-width is a geometric limit) and it also has the highest AUC, so the choice is partly after the fact. It separates M from S, not all failures from S: it says nothing about the grasp classes (which have no meeting) and nothing about class 4a (AUC 0.553 against S).

[R1] Corrections to the list of numbers tried. (1) "The highest AUC" holds on the design seeds only: on the socket grid the rejected centre-based offset pre_lat has AUC 0.778 against 0.658 for the tip number, and on chunk 25 the depth number post_min_tip_ax has 0.9125 against 0.9086. I keep pre_tip_lat (chosen on physical grounds and because it had the highest design AUC, so partly after the fact), and the two runs above show it is not the best number everywhere. (2) A number 20 steps before contact, pre_tip_lat_20, was also tried and dropped: AUC 0.548 on the design seeds (0.612 chunk 50, 0.696 chunk 25, 0.841 ensembling, 0.418 and 0.467 on the grids), so 0.4 s earlier M and S are not separable. (3) The radial tip offset (hypot of the two) gives M vs S 0.835, M vs S and A 0.797, A vs S 0.553 (now coded, `radial_tip_offset_auc_design`). (4) The tip number is defined only for episodes that meet (593 of 1000). An edit that changes which episodes meet changes the sample, so any Stage 2 or 3 table must report the met count (and the met set) beside it. The radial and tip versions were chosen after looking at the same seeds, so their AUCs are descriptive.

## Near-miss evidence

Numbers over all of the 405 M episodes against all 74 strict successes.

- Scale. One step before contact, half of the strict successes are within 0.8 cm of the axis (median 0.80), the physical limit, against 4.4% of M (median 2.05, p10 to p90 1.03 to 3.29). 48.9% of M lie above the success p90 of 2.08 cm (about 10% of the S group is by definition above it).
- Direction. The signed offsets across the axis are similar in the two groups, about zero or a half cm in mean (y: S -0.57, M -0.49; z: S 0.13, M -0.09), but the scatter is wider in M (sd 1.80 and 1.78 against 0.95 and 0.97). So it is a wider spread around the target, not a common offset in one direction that an output-level constant bias could remove. (A constant bias in the average is small.) I did not measure whether an individual episode's error is predictable from its start, beyond the position tests below.
- Time course. Both groups start close to each other at 60 steps before contact (5.8 and 5.6 cm median tip offset) and 20 steps before contact are 1.6 and 1.9 cm. In the last 20 steps the successes close in to 0.80, while the M episodes stay at 1.8 to 2.0 (timecourse in `ins_pattern.json`, `time_course_default_tip_lat_cm_median`: S 1.56, 0.90, 0.80, 0.80 and M 1.87, 1.79, 1.95, 2.05 at 20, 10, 5 and 1 steps before contact). I take this as descriptive: M does not converge in the last 0.4 s. I cannot say from it whether the aim is wrong or the grasp already was.
- Depth [R1, corrected]. The earlier figures (the tip past the mouth by 5 mm in 40% of M, 15 mm in 25%) had no lateral condition, so a peg sliding past beside the socket counted as inside. They are withdrawn. The opening is 1.8 cm half-wide across and the pin fills 4 cm from the socket centre, so a tip inside the opening cannot get below about 4 cm without pin contact. M has none of it: 77 of 405 have an ungated deepest tip below 3.8 cm, all outside the opening. Counting with the lateral condition at the deepest step (tip centre offset below 1.8 cm) instead: of the 163 M episodes whose deepest tip passed 5 mm beyond the mouth plane, 22 (5% of M) were in the opening and 141 (35% of M) were at 1.8 cm or more from the axis, beside it. Only 1 passed 15 mm in the opening (100 outside). Four corners of the leading face inside the opening gives 21 (5 mm) and 1 (15 mm). For the successes and A, every one of the 74 and 114 got past 5 mm with the tip in the opening (60 and 90 past 15 mm). The median deepest tip of M is 5.79 cm and the p10 2.36 cm (S 3.995 to 4.67, A 4.06 to 4.66 cm p10 to p90), which the first version left out. The same counts for other runs, M passed beside and in the opening: chunk 50 116 and 44 of 415, peg grid 249 and 22 of 722, socket grid 330 and 71 of 805.
- What M does at the mouth [R1]. 242 of 405 (60%) keep their deepest tip within 5 mm of the mouth plane (5.5 cm or more from the socket centre), with tip lateral offset there below 0.8 cm in 18, 0.8 to 1.8 cm in 119 and above 1.8 cm in 105. The other 163 passed the plane, 141 of them beside the opening. So M is a mixture of blocked at the rim, beside, and aligned but not entering (the 18 and the 22).
- Near miss [R1]. Restated on these numbers: before contact 4.4% of M are within the 0.8 cm limit and 18% within 1.2 cm, 33% within 1.6 cm and 62% within 2.4 cm (`near_miss_shares_design`). At depth, 5% enter the opening. I no longer call M a near miss in depth. The median offset (2.05 cm) is about 2.5 times the limit, and the miss is coarse, not a near miss in the sense used for transfer cube. A better near miss by the data is class 4a (the target paragraph, [R1]): median longest strict run 14 of 25 steps, 29 of 114 reached 20 steps.
- Timing. M meets later than S (median step 304 against 286), and 20% of M meet in the last 50 steps of the 400-step episode (A 17.5%, S 0%). A late meet leaves less time. I did not test how many of the M would succeed with more time.
- Decision age. Meets after the step-300 decision (the latest replan) have a slightly smaller tip offset in M (2.00 cm against 2.08 before step 300), in S 0.60 against 0.87 and in A 0.73 against 1.14 [R1, the S and A numbers were left out]. Fewer meets in S after step 300 (19 of 74, M 215 of 405). The change in M is small compared with its size, so this does not point to a stale open-loop plan as the cause of M. The chunk-50 result (below) is in the same direction, small.

What this does not show: that a better tip alignment would lead to an insertion (S includes only 50% of episodes within the tolerance, and S can succeed when the tip is 2 cm off one step before contact, because contact can push the peg in), or any mechanism in the network. Near miss in M means only that the tip offset is about 2.5 times the success median (2.05 against 0.80 cm), with the depth picture above.

## Pattern against start position

Spec table: grows with distance from the centre, one error everywhere, a zone, or no pattern. The data here: no growth with distance for the tracking number, a zone-like structure, and [R1] a deterministic dependence on position that is not linear and is partly smooth at short range (the earlier "noise" reading is withdrawn below).

- Distance from the box centre: the tip offset does not depend on it (Spearman 0.01 for the peg, -0.01 for the socket, on the design seeds). The SPEC reach-error test is a little different: the reach deviation of the right gripper at the first close rises with the peg's distance (median by distance fifth, all episodes, 1.64, 1.68, 1.81, 2.11, 2.42 cm, Spearman 0.14, and 0.26 among the 74 strict successes), and of the left gripper with the socket's (1.38 to 2.45, 0.28 and 0.34). The rise is about 1 cm from the centre to the edge. The same size of rise is not visible in the tracking number, and the reach number is a mixture of M, A and G episodes with very different values (median 1.0 cm S, 1.4 M, 2.3 to 3.0 cm in classes 0 and 1), so the gain pattern is at most a part of the picture.
- Position dependence that does exist: smaller peg x goes with a larger tip offset (Spearman -0.29 on design, -0.32 on the peg grid, median by fifth of peg x: 2.4, 2.1, 1.4, 1.4, 1.4 cm) and socket y (-0.11 design, -0.19 on the socket grid, median by fifth 1.9, 1.6, 1.7, 1.8, 1.5 cm design). Moderate and in different directions on the two objects. [R1] Peg y was left out and is stronger than socket y: Spearman +0.21 on design and +0.21 on the peg grid (socket y -0.11 and -0.19). Median tip offset by fifth of peg y 1.26, 1.33, 2.04, 2.03, 1.86 cm on design, 1.73, 1.49, 1.37, 2.15, 2.92 cm on the peg grid.
- Linear model of the signed tip offset on the four start coordinates: R2 0.09 (y) and 0.05 (z) on design, 0.05 and 0.15 on the peg grid and 0.01 and 0.00 on the socket grid. [R1] This shows the dependence is not linear, not that it is absent. Policy and physics are deterministic, so there is no noise at a fixed position, and the sentence "most of the scatter is within-position noise" is withdrawn.
- Local structure [R1]. On the 41 by 41 grids, the signed tip offset of a met episode is correlated with that of the neighbouring grid point 2.5 mm away in x: 0.61 (y) and 0.71 (z) on the peg grid, 0.60 and 0.65 on the socket grid (half the mean squared difference over the variance 0.39, 0.29, 0.38, 0.34). 5 mm in y it is 0.24 to 0.44, and 2 cm away (lag 8) in x it is 0.09 to 0.36, in y about zero (-0.07 to 0.16). The leave-one-out k-nearest-neighbour R2 (k 4, 8, 16, box-scaled) is 0.33, 0.27, 0.23 (y) and 0.45, 0.39, 0.35 (z) on the peg grid and 0.40, 0.40, 0.38 (y) and 0.47, 0.44, 0.42 (z) on the socket grid. On the design seeds, where the four coordinates vary together and the median nearest neighbour is 0.25 of a box half-width away, the same R2 is at most 0.17 for the signed offset (k 16) and 0.24 to 0.29 for the absolute tip offset (k 4 to 32). So the offset has position structure with a length scale of roughly 1 to 2 cm, finer than a linear model can fit and too fine to show on the 4-D design seeds. Neither the linear R2 nor the 4-D kNN R2 alone says whether a head edit could use it.
- Zones (M share of all rollouts in 5 by 5 cells, `ins_pattern.json` `cells`, also drawn in `failure_map.png`). Peg grid: 0% and 8% in the two low-x cells (peg x 0.10 to 0.12 and 0.12 to 0.14, y 0.44 to 0.48), up to 78% in the two middle-x cells at y 0.52 to 0.56 (x 0.14 to 0.18), and 17 to 49% in the top row (y 0.56 to 0.60). In the `failure_map.png` grid the band at peg y 0.46 to 0.50 is mostly the grasp classes, and M appears in most other places. Socket grid: up to 89% at x -0.16 to -0.14, y 0.52 to 0.56, and 5 to 6% in the corner cells with x -0.12 to -0.10 (y 0.56 to 0.60 and y 0.40 to 0.44). In the rightmost column (x -0.12 to -0.10) the M share by y from low to high is 6, 43, 25, 72 and 5%, so there is no single edge pattern.
- Clustering: given a seed in M, its nearest design seed (4-D, scaled by the box sizes) is in M 55% of the time, against a base rate of 40.5%. [R1] For seeds not in M, the nearest neighbour is in M 32% of the time (the earlier "32% for seeds with a non-M neighbour" stated this the wrong way around). For the grasp classes (0 to 2) 62% and 26% (base rate 40.7%), so both are clustered, the grasp classes more.
- [R1] The closest reading of the SPEC table is "error only inside one zone, with several zones, plus a position structure finer than 2 cm". The distance pattern is weak and the constant-bias reading is not supported. The last row of SPEC's table ("no pattern, noise, probably not editable") is not supported either, because the structure is deterministic and partly smooth. I cannot tell from these numbers whether an edit can exploit a structure with a length scale of 1 to 2 cm (a head's effect is global in the input, not on that scale), so no editability claim is made.

Demo overlay: the 50 training demos' first-frame object positions (from `demo_objects.json`, residual rms 0.3 mm, all inside both boxes) are drawn on the maps (`failure_map.png`). I did not quantify failure against distance to the nearest demo.

## Primary metric

Strict success (7.4%). The gap between benchmark success and strict success is 11.4 points (18.8% minus 7.4%), above the 10-point swap rule, so strict becomes primary and benchmark is the guard. `python -m referee.recompute decisions --task insertion` gives the same (strict, 100, proceed). The strict rule is the insertion definition in DEVIATIONS.md (2026-11-25, written before any insertion rollout), not SPEC's cube definition. Its variants on the design seeds are logged beside it: the depth-based version 62 of 1000 and the both-fingers version 22 of 1000, against 74 for the one-finger rule. All of them keep the gap above 10 points (12.6 and 16.6 points), so the decision does not depend on the reading of "holds".

## Operating point

100 steps. Chunk gains on the strict score: chunk 50 +2.8 points (102 against 74 of 1000, 76 fixed and 48 broken), chunk 25 -4.5 points (29). The rule needs +3 on the primary score, so 100 stays. This is close: on the benchmark score chunk 50 would gain exactly +3.0 (218 against 188, 132 fixed, 102 broken), which is the margin. The rule reads the primary score and I follow it as written. Ensembling at 0.01 on the first 200 design seeds (LeRobot's ensembler in my harness, not compared with the stock evaluator) scores 12 strict (6.0%) against 19 on the same 200 at default (9.5%), 6 fixed and 13 broken. Proceed is true: strict fails 92.6%.

## Known weaknesses

- The design seeds are used for the target choice, the tracking number, the thresholds and the classes' reading, with no correction for the number of looks. The grids are the same policy on other positions, and the peg-grid and socket-grid pictures differ, so a claim that holds on one and fails on the other should be treated as weak.
- The tracking number does not cover the 407 grasp failures (about 44% of all strict failures) and does not separate class 4a. If an edit changes the grasp stage, this number will not see it. [R1] The pooled reach AUCs (0.749 and 0.746) hide a clean split: the right arm's misses (214 seeds, 0.945 against S) and the left arm's (120 seeds, 0.985). They are not the target because a rescued grasp episode is worth about 0.11 strict (local estimate, untested), but if a candidate changes the grasp classes, their counts must be reported, split by arm.
- The tip-offset AUC on the socket grid is 0.66 (S median 1.34 cm). I do not know why the socket-moved grid behaves differently. A possible cause is that the peg is always at its box centre there, which I did not check.
- The strict class "S" is n = 74 on the design seeds. Medians and AUCs of S come from 74 episodes (and 12 in the ensembling run).
- A weight edit for M has no obvious mechanism in these numbers: the tip offset is position structure at a 1 to 2 cm scale (not linear) and the time course suggests it is set in the last half second before contact. [R1] I did not separate arm tracking error, grasp pose and command error, and I did not check which of the chunks (at steps 200 or 300) commands the approach. A deeper decomposition would need the commanded trajectory, which I have not computed.
- "Ceiling": with strict 7.4%, a large win in the verdict rule needs a net fix of 25% of the strict failures of the test set, which is hard to reach by a head edit. I only record this, I do not change any rule.
- [R1] The stage arithmetic (0.11 and 0.39 per rescue, and the stage bounds) assumes the rescued episodes behave like the episodes that already pass that stage. Not tested.
- [R1] The depth, run and reach numbers come from per-step traces that are not in git. Per-episode outputs are committed (`depth_*`, `runs_default`, `reach_*default`), and a reviewer can check them but cannot rebuild them without the cache. The commanded-aim check was done for the two grasps only, not for the approach to the socket, and the target's failure mechanism before contact is unresolved.
- [R1] 81 of 405 M meet at step 350 or later and 25 of 114 A runs end at the end of the episode, so some fraction of these are time-limited. I have not tested what extra steps would do (the episode is fixed at 400).
- Frame strips are low resolution and no claim in this file uses them (`strips_notes.md`).
